\subsection{Trained Disparity Estimation Model}\label{trained-disparity}
We did some limited testing with implementing and training our own disparity estimation model, which would have been the \mono{eval6} model setup, but we decided against including these results in our main evaluation because we ran out of space in the paper. With our depth estimation model, StereoDiffusion outperforms the baseline and almost every other setup (except for the groung truth SPSMD setups) in both mean quality (\reffig{fig:eval6-quality-left-mean}) and stability (\reffig{fig:eval6-quality-left-std}) for left image reconstruction. In contrast, the generation quality of the right image is both worse (\refsec{fig:eval6-quality-right-mean}) and varies significantly (\refsec{fig:eval6-quality-right-std}) compared to standard StereoDiffusion. Nevertheless, these results show that either (1) our disparity estimation overfits to our dataset or (2) just using better disparity estimation can significantly improve the reconstruction capability of StereoDiffusion. Future work would certainly verify either of these assumptions, but alas...

\subsection{Attention Weights of Guidance Prompts}\label{attn-weights}
\reffig{fig:attn-maps} shows the aggregated attention scores of a conditioning prompt for a sample from our dataset. The cross-attention module seems to be able to faithfully seperate the concepts for e.g. the tokens \emph{car} and \emph{museum} in the image, while both uni- and bi-directional attention seem to not attend to a particular object in the image for the same tokens. This indicates that uni-/bi-directional attention may be unsuitable for conditioning with null-text inversion, although our evaluation currently does not support this assumption as the mean generation quality of \mono{eval3} is not significantly higher than that of \mono{eval4} (see \reffig{fig:quality-right-mean}).
