\section{Methods}\label{methods}
% intro stereodiffusion
\textbf{StereoDiffusion} \citep{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} is a method for generating stereo image pairs from a single left-side image. The left image is transformed into latent space and then shifted based on its disparity map. The original and shifted latent representations are denoised seperately by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion \citep{rombach2022highresolutionimagesynthesislatent} model into the output left and right stereo image respectively. In the following, we will give a brief overview of the major components of StereoDiffusion. For a more detailed explanation, see \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/StereoDiffusion_overview.png}
    \caption{Overview of the StereoDiffusion architecture as proposed by \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}.}\label{fig:sd_architecture}
\end{figure}

\reffig{fig:sd_architecture} shows an overview of the StereoDiffusion architecture. Let $X^\text{Left}$ denote a left-side stereo image. First a latent representation $Z_t^\text{Left}$ of $X^\text{Left}$ is obtained by inverting the image with a null-text inversion (\cite{mokady2023null}, see also \refsec{null-text-inv}) technique for Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM, \cite{song2020denoising}) sampling \citep{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}. $Z_t^\text{Left}$ is shifted via the \emph{Stereo Pixels Shift} (SPS) operation $\mathcal{S}$ to obtain a latent representation $Z_t^\text{Right}$ for each point $(i,j)$ in the image space given by 
\begin{align}\label{eq:sps}
    Z_t^\text{Right}(i,j)=\mathcal{S}(Z_t^\text{Left},D_{X^\text{Left}},s,i,j)=Z_t^\text{Left}(i-sD_{X^\text{Left}}(i,j),j)
\end{align}
where $D_{X^\text{Left}}$ is the disparity map of $X^\text{Left}$ and $s$ is a uniform scaling factor that controls the strength of the stereo effect (following \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}, we use $s=0.8$ for all our experiments). For StereoDiffusion, $D_{X^\text{Left}}$ is estimated with DPT \citep{ranftl2021visiontransformersdenseprediction} but might also be obtained with other methods, such as our \emph{Disparity Computation} (see \refsec{depth-to-disparity}) method. \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} found that after shifting, the right-side image becomes inconsistent with the left image, especially in areas with large shifting of the subject matter, such as object outlines close to the cameras. This leads to artifacts and shifted areas being interpreted differently during subsequent denoising \citep{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}. To address this, \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} propose a \emph{Symmetric Pixel Shift Masking Denoise} (SPSMD) operation that is applied at regular intervals $t'$ during the denoising process: given $Z_{t'-1}^\text{Left}$, $Z_{t'}^\text{Right}$ and a mask $M$ that marks the areas where the pixel shift is applied, the right latent is modified to obtain a new latent $Z_{t'}^{'\text{Right}}$ with
\begin{align}\label{eq:spsmd}
    Z_{t'}^{'\text{Right}}(i,j) = 
    \begin{cases}
        \mathcal{S}(Z_{t'-1}^\text{Left},D_{X^\text{Left}},s,i,j) &\text{if }M(i,j)=\text{True}\\
        Z_{t'}^\text{Right}(i,j) &\text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{align}
The thus modified latent stereo images $Z_{t}^\text{Left}$ and $Z_{t}^{'\text{Right}}$ are fed back into the denoising process of Stable Diffusion until the last latent representations $Z_0^\text{Left}$ and $Z_0^\text{Right}$ are generated, which are then decoded into the output stereo image pair $Y^\text{Left},Y^\text{Right}$.

In this work we investigate what impact different alterations of this architecture have on its ability to generate stereo image pairs. Specifically, we propose and evaluate the following modifications to StereoDiffusion: (1) a method for calculating the disparity map from an estimated depth map and sensor data (\refsec{depth-to-disparity}), (2) utilizing cross-attention \citep{rombach2022highresolutionimagesynthesislatent,vaswani-2017-transformer} instead of uni-/bi-directional attention \citep{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} (\refsec{cross-attn}), (3) incorporating prompt-based image editing via null-text inversion \citep{mokady2023null} (\refsec{null-text-inv}) and (4) aligning the generated right-side stereo image with the ground truth instead of the generated left image (\refsec{gt-alignment}).

\subsection{Disparity Computation}\label{depth-to-disparity}
Given stereo images with parallel optical axes, the disparity map $D$ can be derived from a depth map $Z$ using
\begin{align}\label{eq:disparity}
    D(i,j)=\frac{fB}{Z(i,j)}
\end{align}
where $(i,j)$ is an arbitrary point in image space, $f$ the focal length and $B$ the baseline distance between the two stereo images \citep{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion}. Since our dataset incorporates extensive sensor data (see \refsec{dataset}), including focal length and baseline distance of the cameras that were used to generate the ground truth images, we can leverage this data and only have to estimate the depth map for the computation of the disparity map. This allows us to obtain an alternative disparity map $D_\text{comp}$ with
\begin{align}\label{eq:new-disparity}
    D_\text{comp}(i,j)=\frac{fB}{Z_{DPT}(i,j)}
\end{align}
where $f$ and $B$ are the focal length and baseline distance from our dataset respectively and $Z_{DPT}$ is a depth map estimated with a pretrained DPT model\footnote{\mono{dpt$\_$hybrid-midas-501f0c75}} \citep{ranftl2021visiontransformersdenseprediction,ranftl2020midas}. During inference, we simply replace the original DPT-estimated $D_{X^\text{Left}}$ in \refeq{eq:sps} with $D_\text{comp}$. We term this the Disparity Computation method.  

\subsection{Cross-Attention}\label{cross-attn}
In addition to the novel uni-/bi-directional attention proposed by \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} for StereoDiffusion, we did test using cross-attention which is commonly used for Stable Diffusion-based image generation \citep{rombach2022highresolutionimagesynthesislatent,hertz2022prompt,mokady2023null}. This is because we also evaluate StereoDiffusion with prompt-based conditioning of the generation process (see \refsec{null-text-inv}) and during testing we noticed that uni-/bi-directional attention does not preserve the attention weights of the conditioning prompt (see \refsec{attn-weights} for an example).

\subsection{Null-text Inversion}\label{null-text-inv}
Null-text inversion \citep{mokady2023null} is a method for text-guided image editing. In the context of image editing with a generative diffusion model $\varepsilon_\theta$ (such as Stable Diffusion), this means that given an image $X$ and a text prompt $\mathcal{P}$, the model should generate an image $X'$ that is different from $X$, such that
\begin{align}
    X\neq X' = D(Z_0)\text{ with }Z_0&=\varepsilon_\theta(Z_T,\mathcal{C}) \\
        Z_T&=E(X) \\
        \mathcal{C}&=\psi(\mathcal{P})
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{C}$ is the embedding of $\mathcal{P}$ and $E,D$ are the image encoder and decoder respectively (see also \cite{mokady2023null}). To this end, \cite{mokady2023null} leverage a method for text-guided editing called classifier-free guidance (CFG, \cite{ho2022classifier}) that is used to amplify the influence of the text condition $\mathcal{P}$. CFG performs the diffusion process twice, once with the text condition and once unconditionally with a \emph{null text} embedding $\emptyset=\psi("")$, modifying $\varepsilon_\theta$ to obtain $\overline{\varepsilon}_\theta$ with
\begin{align}\label{eq:cfg}
    \overline{\varepsilon}_\theta(Z_T,\mathcal{C},\emptyset)=w\cdot\varepsilon_\theta(Z_T,\mathcal{C})+(1-w)\cdot\varepsilon_\theta(Z_T,\emptyset)
\end{align}
where $w$ is the guidance scale parameter. In comparison to standard CFG, \cite{mokady2023null} achieve accurate reconstruction of the input image while preserving the editability enabled by CFG by optimizing the null text embedding $\emptyset$ such that the unconditional backward pass accurately reconstructs the input image. For StereoDiffusion, \cite{Wang-2024-stereodiffusion} use this optimized unconditional diffusion to obtain the initial latent representation of the input left-side stereo image $Z_T^\text{Left}$. An important consideration when using null-text inversion is that the optimization, which has to be done for each image individually adds around 10-30s per image on a single A100 GPU which currently limits the usefulness of this approach for large-scale applications.

Since null-text inversion was originally proposed for image editing, we did test using it to influence the generation of the right-side stereo image by passing a conditioning prompt of the form
\begin{lstlisting}
    "[Half-sentence describing the scene and depicted object], captured with a stereo camera with baseline distance B and focal length f"
\end{lstlisting}
where \mono{[...]}, \mono{B} and \mono{f} are replaced during inference with the sample-specific values.

\subsection{Ground-truth SPSMD}\label{gt-alignment}
The Symmetric Pixel Shift Masking Denoise module (see \refeq{eq:spsmd}) copies values from the last $(t-1)$-th latent left image to the current $t$-th latent right image every few time steps (we used every $10$ out of the $50$ total DDIM sampling steps) to increase the alignment of the two images. But, since by design we also have the ground truth left image available (as it is the input for the diffusion model), we do not have to align the right with the generated left (as this is only an approximation of the ground truth, see also \cite{mokady2023null}), we can instead skip generating the left image altogether and align the generated right with the ground truth left image directly. To implement this idea we modified the SPSMD module like so
\begin{align}\label{eq:gt-align}
    Z_{t'}^{'\text{Right}}(i,j) &= 
    \begin{cases}
        \mathcal{S}(Z_{t'-1}^\text{gt},D_{X^\text{Left}},s,i,j) &\text{if }M(i,j)=\text{True}\\
        Z_{t'}^\text{Right}(i,j) &\text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}\\
        \text{with }&Z_{t'-1}^\text{gt} = \epsilon_{\theta,:t'-1}(E(X^\text{Left}),\emptyset)
\end{align}
where $\epsilon_{\theta,:t'-1}$ is the forward diffusion process up to time step $t'-1$. The output of this modified version of StereoDiffusion then becomes $X^\text{Left},Y^\text{Right}$ (which is why in the evaluation (\reffig{fig:left-vs-right-ssim}) the similarity of the output left with the ground truth left image for \mono{eval7} and \mono{eval8} is exactly 1).
